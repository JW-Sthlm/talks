The economics of the work changed.

Why this is happening now

  • 280× cheaper to run the AI reasoning behind that work in two years
  • >25% faster through the same consulting task list
  • >40% higher quality, scored by human graders

Price: Stanford HAI AI Index using Epoch AI data, November 2022 to October 2024. Performance: Dell'Acqua, Lakhani and others, Harvard Digital Data Design Institute with Boston Consulting Group, 2023, on 758 consultants and 2023 models.

1:30 calm Economics · what changed underneath

DO
Do not explain tokens. Land the middle two numbers, they are about your audience's job.

So why now, and not three years ago?

Two things moved. The cost of running machine reasoning fell by a factor of two hundred and eighty in two years. You do not need the technical detail behind that. It means work that was once too expensive to touch became work nobody thinks twice about.

The other two numbers matter more to you, because they were measured on your job.

Harvard and BCG took seven hundred and fifty-eight consultants and randomly assigned them to work with or without a frontier model on eighteen realistic consulting tasks. Over twenty-five percent faster. Over forty percent better, scored by human graders.

Be honest about the timeline here. Both numbers are older than they look. The price figure stops at the end of 2024 and has kept falling since. The study ran in 2023 on models two generations behind today, so treat the exact percentages loosely. The direction has not reversed.

AI works across the whole assignment.

Across the whole engagement

2:00 calm AI across the engagement 6 clicks

DO
Click through the five phases. Do not read every cell. Click once more for the bottom line, land it and stop.

People hear AI and picture one task, a summary or a draft email. That is too narrow. It runs across the whole assignment.

Prepare: it reads the context and shows what matters for you to decide.

Interview: it transcribes, highlights contradictions and keeps you focused while you are still in the room.

Analyse: it preserves the evidence and sources so you can judge what is good enough.

Recommend: it drafts options and trade-offs. You make the call.

Present: it prepares questions and follow-up. You take the pushback.

The split stays simple. The volume goes. The judgement stays, and the recommendation keeps your name on it.

07 / 18

When the delivery model changes, the business model changes with it.

Hard truths

1:45 high Commercial model 2 clicks

DO
Read the left panel first. Then the commercial panel. Pause on capabilities compounding.

The delivery model is already changing the business model.

If a pre-study takes half the calendar time, you cannot keep pricing it the same way and expect the model to hold.

The delivery team changes first. A smaller senior team directs a fleet of agents instead of a pyramid doing the assembly work.

Then the economics change. Compressed timelines force you to price the outcome rather than the hours. Prompts, methods and agents improve between deliveries. That is IP. It compounds.

The relationship does not have to stop when the work is delivered. A retainer keeps the evidence, benchmarks and advisory work moving.

This is not a blueprint every firm should copy. But the old model cannot remain the default once the delivery engine changes. Decide how you will capture that compounding IP: retainer, outcome pricing or licensing.

Point back to the conflict Scout could not resolve on its own. Keep this practical and personal.

Scout stopped a minute ago because leadership, operations and market evidence pointed one way and challenge pointed another. That pause is not a limitation to apologise for. It is the point.

If the machine carries the volume, what exactly are you being paid for?

Three things. These are the expensive ones.

Context. What this client can actually absorb, and what the board will refuse to hear this quarter. No model in the room knows that.

Quality. Which evidence is strong enough to carry the firm's name. That judgement is about risk. A statement can be technically accurate and still be unsafe to use.

And the call. You own the recommendation itself, together with the risk of being wrong in front of a steering group with the CFO in it.

That is why the stop in the demo matters. A confident answer is cheap. Knowing when the evidence needs a human call is the valuable part.

1:15 calm Methodology that runs

DO
Move clockwise. Keep this about consulting IP, not model training. The correction you just chose on the conflict is the raw material for this loop.

Consulting firms have always had IP: method decks, interview guides, benchmark databases, quality checklists. Useful, but passive. Somebody still has to remember where those assets are and apply them properly.

Agents change the form of that IP.

Project work produces evidence, decisions and outcomes. The choice you just watched, one operating model with two exceptions, becomes a correction. Those corrections become better instructions, stronger examples and tighter checks. The next engagement starts from a reviewed capability instead of an empty chat window.

The model is not secretly learning from client data. The firm is deliberately improving an asset it controls.

That makes IP more important. When everybody can generate another app or another analysis, the advantage is the delivery system that improves every time your people review it.

That is how AI-driven delivery becomes a competitive advantage. The next team does not start from scratch. It starts from what the firm already learned.
