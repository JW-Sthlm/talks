Hard truths

When the delivery model changes,
the business model changes with it.

The delivery team

Smaller team. More capacity.

  • From a pyramid to a lead and a fleet of agents. Fewer people doing the assembly work.
  • Capabilities compound. The prompts, methods and agents improve from one delivery to the next.
The commercial model

Price the outcome, then keep earning.

  • Compressed timelines change the unit you sell. Price the answer, not the hours.
  • IP compounds across deliveries. Turn the method into a reusable advantage.
  • Retainers keep the work moving. Ongoing evidence, benchmarks and advisory after the pack lands.
DO: Read the left panel first. Then the commercial panel. Pause on capabilities compounding. SAY: The delivery model is already changing the business model. If a pre-study takes half the calendar time, you cannot keep pricing it the same way and expect the model to hold. The delivery team changes first. A smaller senior team directs a fleet of agents instead of a pyramid doing the assembly work. Then the economics change. Compressed timelines force you to price the outcome rather than the hours. Prompts, methods and agents improve between deliveries. That is IP. It compounds. The relationship does not have to stop when the work is delivered. A retainer keeps the evidence, benchmarks and advisory work moving. This is not a blueprint every firm should copy. But the old model cannot remain the default once the delivery engine changes. Decide how you will capture that compounding IP: retainer, outcome pricing or licensing.
